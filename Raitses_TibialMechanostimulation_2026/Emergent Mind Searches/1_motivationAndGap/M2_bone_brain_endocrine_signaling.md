# Mechanotransduction and Endocrine Signaling in the Bone-Brain Axis

Mechanical stimulation of long bones, such as dynamic axial tibial loading, induces fluid shear stresses across the osteocyte lacuno-canalicular network (OLCN), triggering biochemical signaling cascades that extend beyond local skeletal remodeling. Osteocytes act as mechanosensory hubs that release endocrine factors into systemic circulation, thereby engaging the central nervous system via the bone-brain axis. This interaction regulates homeostatic feedback loops, neurogenesis, cognitive function, and energy metabolism through circulating osteokines and neuroendocrine intermediates.

## Mechanosensing in the Osteocyte Lacuno-Canalicular Network

The skeletal response to mechanical deformation relies on the geometry and connectivity of the osteocyte syncytium embedded within the mineralized extracellular matrix. Dynamic tibial compression generates cyclic pressure gradients that drive interstitial fluid through the pericellular space of the lacuno-canalicular system [1702.04117]. Osteocyte cell bodies and dendritic processes experience fluid shear stress (FSS), activating mechanosensitive ion channels (such as Piezo1 and TRPV4) and integrin-based focal adhesions ($α_vβ_3$). 

This mechanical stimulus modulates intracellular calcium signaling, activates adenylyl cyclase to increase cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP), and triggers the immediate release of nitric oxide (NO) and prostaglandin $E_2$ ($\text{PGE}_2$) [1201.0239]. Locally, this mechanotransductive pathway suppresses osteocytic secretion of sclerostin (the product of the *SOST* gene), disinhibiting canonical Wnt/$\beta$-catenin signaling in osteoprogenitor pools to drive bone formation and structural adaptation [1211.6397, 1905.08099].

## Endocrine Secretion and Circulating Osteokines

Beyond orchestrating osteoblast and osteoclast activity within cortical and trabecular basic multicellular units, mechanosensitive osteocytes function as endocrine cells capable of modulating distant organ systems:

- **Fibroblast Growth Factor 23 (FGF23)**: Primarily synthesized by osteocytes, circulating intact FGF23 (iFGF23) acts via $\alpha$-Klotho-FGFR complexes in renal proximal tubules to regulate phosphate wasting and suppresses $1\alpha$-hydroxylase activity. Mechanical loading modulates FGF23 transcription and cleavage pathways, influencing systemic mineral metabolism and neurovascular tone.
- **Osteocalcin (OCN)**: In response to cyclic strain and bone turnover, osteoblasts and embedding osteocytes release osteocalcin, an osteogenic marker sensitive to biomechanical stress [2001.08945]. In its uncarboxylated form (uOCN), it crosses vascular barriers to exert endocrine control over systemic glucose homeostasis and neural targets.
- **Lipocalin-2 (LCN2)**: Osteocyte-derived LCN2 functions as an appetite-suppressing hormone and acts on the central nervous system via the melanocortin-4 receptor (MC4R) pathway in the hypothalamus.
- **Extracellular Vesicles (EVs) and OsteomiRs**: Mechanical strain stimulates the exocytosis of osteocyte-derived small extracellular vesicles containing bioactive microRNAs (such as miR-214 and miR-29 families) and osteokines into the circulation, providing protected vascular delivery to distal tissues.

## Mediating the Bone-Brain Axis

Circulating factors mobilized by mechanical stimulation traverse or interact directly with the blood-brain barrier (BBB) to modulate neuroendocrine circuits and hippocampal physiology. Uncarboxylated osteocalcin crosses the BBB, binding to G-protein coupled receptor class C group 6 member A (GPRC6A) receptors localized in the hippocampus, ventral tegmental area, and brainstem. This signaling event promotes the expression of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), enhances monoaminergic neurotransmitter synthesis (serotonin, dopamine, noradrenaline), and facilitates adult hippocampal neurogenesis, thereby improving cognitive and spatial memory consolidation.

Conversely, central hypothalamic circuits exert centrifugal control over skeletal homeostasis via the sympathetic nervous system (SNS) and the hypothalamic-pituitary axis. Elevated central leptin or sympathetic outflow stimulates $\beta_2$-adrenergic receptors ($\beta_2\text{-AR}$) on osteocytes and osteoblasts, suppressing osteogenesis and dampening mechanical sensitivity. Local tibial loading counteracts this tone by downregulating peripheral adrenergic sensitivity and altering local skeletal perfusion. In parallel, mechanical stimulation can activate afferent skeletal sensory neurons (expressing $\text{TrkA}$ and calcitonin gene-related peptide, CGRP), relaying mechanical strain data through dorsal root ganglia to ascending central circuits, which coordinate autonomic feedback and neuroimmune modulation.

## Conclusion

Dynamic tibial mechanical loading activates osteocytic fluid-flow shear transduction pathways, leading to the systemic secretion of endocrine osteokines including uncarboxylated osteocalcin, FGF23, and vesicular microRNAs. These circulating factors access central neural substrates to regulate neurogenesis, neurotransmitter balance, and metabolic homeostasis across the bidirectional bone-brain axis. Mapping the molecular kinetics of this load-induced endocrine network remains crucial for developing mechanical and biomimetic therapies targeting age-related neurodegenerative and musculoskeletal decline.
