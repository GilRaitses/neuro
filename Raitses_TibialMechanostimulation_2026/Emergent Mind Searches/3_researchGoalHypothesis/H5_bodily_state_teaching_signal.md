# Neuromodulated Sensorimotor Integration and Dopaminergic Plasticity

In closed-loop motor control, bodily state estimation and proprioceptive feedback do not simply provide passive coordinates; they supply essential teaching signals that govern associative synaptic plasticity. Proprioceptive afferents encode limb kinematics and musculoskeletal forces, forming the predictive baseline against which efference copies and sensory feedback are compared. When these sensorimotor contingencies are modulated by phasic dopamine—acting both as a reward prediction error (RPE) and an action-surprise signal—corticostriatal circuits modify synaptic efficacy via three-factor learning rules. The following sections examine how proprioceptive state estimation interacts with dopaminergic neuromodulation and associative plasticity to drive adaptive motor learning.

## Proprioceptive Feedback and Internal State Estimation

Motor learning relies on resolving the transformation between internal motor commands and subsequent bodily states. Proprioceptive afferents, primarily from muscle spindles and Golgi tendon organs, convey continuous variables regarding muscle stretch velocity, length, and joint loading to the spinal cord, primary somatosensory cortex (S1), and posterior parietal cortex (PPC) [1607.05944]. Because nonlinear musculoskeletal biomechanics preclude trivial one-to-one mappings between joint angles and fascicle lengths, neural populations must build internal forward models through exploratory babbling and closed-loop feedback. 

To compensate for axonal conduction and processing delays that inherently destabilize biological feedback controllers, the central nervous system deploys predictive coding architectures. Efference copies of descending corticospinal drive are integrated with internal sensory feedback, allowing fast forward models to cancel self-generated sensory consequences and isolate unexpected bodily deviations [2211.05922]. Within computational implementations, recursive variational inference minimizes free energy across multimodal proprioceptive and tactile inputs, updating internal representations of body configuration through continuous prediction error minimization [1805.03104].

## Dopamine as an Extended Teaching Signal in Motor Circuits

Classically formalised as an scalar RPE signaling the discrepancy between expected and received reward ($\delta_t = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)$), midbrain dopaminergic activity within the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNc) and ventral tegmental area (VTA) exhibits complex kinematic and action-dependent dynamics during motor execution. Dopamine transients emerge prior to movement initiation and scale with kinematics such as acceleration and movement vigor, indicating that dopaminergic teaching signals exceed pure reward processing.

Recent computational formulations show that dopamine signals integrate both reward expectations and "action surprise"—a metric reflecting discrepancies between an executed motor action and the baseline policy generated by basal ganglia circuits [2207.00636]. By communicating surprise alongside RPEs, dopaminergic afferents enable off-policy reinforcement learning across parallel motor pathways, permitting the striatum to learn from movements executed under cortical, cerebellar, or reflexive control. Furthermore, dopamine prediction errors actively shape state space representations themselves, structuring neural population geometry in cortical and hippocampal ensembles to support state categorization and spatial-temporal representations [2108.12402].

## Associative Plasticity and Three-Factor Learning Rules

Bridging the temporal gap between millisecond-level spike timing and delayed behavioral outcomes requires three-factor synaptic plasticity rules. In canonical two-factor Hebbian plasticity or spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP), synaptic eligibility traces $\bar{e}_{ij}$ are generated by correlated pre- and postsynaptic activity:

$$\tau_e \frac{d e_{ij}(t)}{dt} = -e_{ij}(t) + f(\text{pre}_i, \text{post}_j)$$

These eligibility traces remain biochemically tagged at dendritic spines—typically through calcium influx via NMDA receptors and downstream kinase/phosphatase cascades—without immediately altering baseline AMPA receptor conductance.

A global teaching signal, delivered via phasic dopamine release, converts these transient eligibility traces into permanent long-term potentiation (LTP) or long-term depression (LTD) [1502.06096, 1704.04238]. Corticostriatal medium spiny neurons (MSNs) exhibit receptor-dependent bidirectionality: D1-expressing direct pathway MSNs promote LTP in the presence of dopamine bursts to reinforce successful motor sequences, whereas D2-expressing indirect pathway MSNs undergo LTD or depotentiation under dopamine elevation to suppress maladaptive motor competing programs [1009.4516, 1906.09290]. At the cortical level, apical dendrites receive predictive feedback that modulates local somatic and basal synaptic plasticity via disinhibitory microcircuits, orchestrating hierarchical credit assignment without requiring symmetric backward connections [1801.00062, 2310.19614].

## Conclusion

Bodily state representations derived from proprioceptive feedback act as dynamic anchors for motor learning, generating the sensory predictions necessary to compute motor errors. When integrated with dopamine-dependent three-factor associative plasticity, these signals gate long-term modifications across corticostriatal networks to optimize behavioral policies. This coupling between predictive bodily state estimation and neuromodulated synaptic adaptation represents a foundational mechanism for robust, delay-tolerant biological control.
