# PIEZO1-Mediated Mechanotransduction and Calcium Signaling in Osteocytes During Tibial Axial Loading

Dynamic in vivo axial loading of the mammalian tibia induces localized cortical strains that drive high-velocity interstitial fluid flow through the porosity of the osteocyte lacuno-canalicular network. This hydrodynamic fluid shear stress elevates lateral membrane tension along osteocyte somata and dendritic processes, opening mechanosensitive PIEZO1 cation channels. Influx of extracellular calcium through gated PIEZO1 activates downstream intracellular calcium-induced calcium release and purinergic signaling, which collectively suppress sclerostin production and elevate osteogenic factor secretion. This response synthesizes the biophysical pathway spanning macroscopic tibial compression, microscopic lacunocanalicular fluid dynamics, PIEZO1 gating mechanics, and downstream anabolic gene transcription.

## Biomechanical Coupling from Axial Tibial Strain to Lacunocanalicular Fluid Flow

Axial cyclic loading applied to the tibia creates non-uniform flexural and compressive strain distributions across the diaphysis. Because bone is a hierarchical poroelastic medium, cyclic tissue-level strains compress the mineralized matrix and induce cyclic hydraulic pressure gradients [1503.01233]. These pressure differentials force interstitial fluid out of the compressed lacunar spaces and through the narrow annular channels surrounding osteocyte processes, forming the lacuno-canalicular network (OLCN) [1702.04117]. 

The nanoscale geometry of the pericellular space—constrained between the canalicular wall and the glycocalyx-tethered osteocyte process—amplifies macroscale matrix deformations into substantial pericellular fluid shear stresses (FSS), typically ranging from $0.5$ to $5\text{ Pa}$. Computational and theoretical models demonstrate that fluid drag against the pericellular matrix and tethering complexes produces both membrane shear stresses and drag-induced axial tensile forces along the cell membrane [1201.0239]. These shear and drag forces perturb the lipid bilayer and cytoskeletal anchors, providing the physical stimulus required to activate mechanosensitive ion channels distributed along the plasma membrane.

## Biophysical Gating of PIEZO1 and Intracellular Calcium Influx

PIEZO1 is a homotrimeric, propeller-shaped mechanosensitive ion channel characterized by non-planar curved transmembrane blades that deform the surrounding lipid bilayer into a characteristic nanoscale dome. Under resting conditions, bilayer curvature stabilizes PIEZO1 in a closed conformation. When fluid shear stress generates in-plane bilayer tension ($\gamma$), the free-energy landscape shifts toward planarization of the curved footprint, expanding the channel's in-plane projected area and driving cooperative transition to the open state [2604.05556]. The membrane tension sensitivity of PIEZO1 allows osteocytes to convert microsecond changes in local cortical fluid velocity directly into electrophysiological conductance changes.

Once opened, PIEZO1 non-selectively conducts monovalent and divalent cations with a pronounced permeability toward $Ca^{2+}$. The immediate inward current ($I_{\text{Piezo1}}$) produces a rapid transient surge in cytosolic free calcium ($[Ca^{2+}]_i$). This localized calcium influx couples with biochemical amplifying pathways, triggering calcium-induced calcium release (CICR) via inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptors ($IP_3R$) and ryanodine receptors on the endoplasmic reticulum membrane [1703.00540]. Furthermore, membrane depolarization downstream of cation flux can recruit secondary voltage-sensitive and non-selective channels, prolonging the intracellular calcium elevation into characteristic oscillatory or sustained waves.

## Paracrine Amplification and Transcriptional Regulation

The initial PIEZO1-mediated $[Ca^{2+}]_i$ transient triggers rapid purinergic paracrine and autocrine signaling cascades throughout the osteocyte syncytium. Elevated intracellular $Ca^{2+}$ promotes opening of connexin 43 (Cx43) hemichannels and pannexin-1 channels, releasing adenosine triphosphate (ATP) into the extracellular lacunar fluid. Extracellular ATP binds adjacent metabotropic (P2Y) and ionotropic (P2X) purinergic receptors, propagating intercellular $Ca^{2+}$ waves across the lacuno-canalicular syncytium to recruit neighboring osteocytes that experience sub-threshold direct fluid shear stress. Concurrently, cytosolic calcium spikes stimulate calcium/calmodulin-dependent pathways that activate endothelial and inducible nitric oxide synthases (eNOS/iNOS) to release nitric oxide (NO), as well as cytosolic phospholipase $A_2$ ($cPLA_2$) to upregulate prostaglandin $E_2$ ($PGE_2$) via cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) [1201.0239].

At the nuclear level, sustained calcium signaling and associated mechanical strains induce the nuclear translocation of key transcriptional co-activators, notably YAP (Yes-associated protein) and TAZ, alongside NFAT dephosphorylation via calcineurin. This transcriptional remodeling orchestrates the primary bone anabolic response by directly downregulating *SOST* gene expression, leading to a marked decrease in sclerostin secretion into the canalicular fluid [1211.6397]. Sclerostin clearance relieves canonical Wnt/$\beta$-catenin pathway inhibition on osteoblasts at the periosteal and endosteal surfaces. In addition, osteocytic expression of receptor activator of nuclear factor-$\kappa$B ligand (RANKL) is suppressed relative to osteoprotegerin (OPG), uncoupling local bone remodeling in favor of osteoblastogenesis and de novo osteoid deposition.

## Conclusion

Tibial axial loading engages osteocyte mechanotransduction by translating whole-bone strains into canalicular fluid shear stresses that gate the mechanosensitive ion channel PIEZO1. Gating of PIEZO1 provides the pivotal initial influx of extracellular $Ca^{2+}$, which subsequently triggers ER-mediated calcium release, purinergic paracrine loops, and downstream Wnt/$\beta$-catenin activation via sclerostin downregulation. Consequently, the osteocyte PIEZO1-$Ca^{2+}$ signaling axis operates as an indispensable mechanosensory transducer governing functional skeletal adaptation to dynamic exercise and physiological loading.
